# Boat ferry riding design

**Status:** Stage 1 built and measured live on 2026-11-18. Builds on `2026-11-17-moving-surface-collision-design.md`, which built the surface layer for the Kelethin lift and reserved `SurfaceKind::Boat`.

## 1. Intent

An agent rides the Freeport to Butcherblock ferry as travel between zones, and can tell from its observation when the ferry is there to board. The client must work on any EQEmu server, so it follows the native client and never depends on edited server data.

**Goals**
- The ferry is visible to the client and to the agent, with its position, heading and motion, so the agent decides when to board.
- A character boards by walking onto the dock where the ferry waits, and the server's own ferry mechanism moves it to the next zone.
- The journey continues the way a native player does, through the translocator in `oot`.

**Non-goals:** boats a player steers (races Boat and Rowboat), boat combat, transports other than ferries.

**Anti-goals:** a rider sent to a safe point with no log line, a zone request the server refuses as an invalid zone line, and a ferry that exists on the server but is silently absent from the client.

## 3. Approaches

1. **Proximity boarding (recommended, stage 1).** Make the ferry visible and observable, and let the agent walk onto the dock. The server's box does the rest. No deck, carry or vehicle wire is needed for this ferry, which also holds for the PEQ ferries that use the same script pattern.
2. **Native faithful deck (stage 2, built in part).** A boat collision surface with a carry, boat-local offsets on the wire and board messages. Stage 2 builds the surface and the carry from the ship mesh (§8). The boat-local wire offsets and board messages stay out of scope, since they matter only for boats the server expects a rider to stand on, such as steerable boats.
3. **Zone-line shortcut.** Rejected. This server has no zone line, and writing one would break other servers.

## 4. Design (stage 1)

- **Keep the ferry.** Registration drops bodytype 11 and 60 spawns, except races that `is_boat_race` names. A boat stays in the entity table with its race, position and heading. The native client renders such ships although the player cannot target them, so the exemption follows it. The entity is marked untargetable. A target, consider or hail that names it is refused locally with the reason `target_untargetable`, `consider_untargetable` or `hail_untargetable`, and no packet goes out. A hail would otherwise reach the server's previous target, since the server fires `EVENT_SAY` on the current target. Every nearest-NPC picker (`interact.hail {}`, the Dialogue and Actions buttons and the click pick) skips it.
- **Agent view.** `VisibleEntity` gains `is_boat: bool` and `velocity: [f32; 3]` in units per second, taken from the last two position updates the way `SurfaceView.velocity` is. A ferry is a `visible` entry sorted by `spawn_id` like any entity, and the agent derives the rest.
  - Present: the ferry appears in `visible`.
  - Absent: it is outside 500 units or out of line of sight, which is what a player sees too. No claim is made about where it is.
  - Docked: speed stays near zero for a sustained time near the dock position, about 60 s for the dock pause. A zero velocity alone does not mean docked, since the ferry reads zero for about 1.5 s at six non-dock waypoints (§6). Leaving: speed rises from zero.
- **Boarding.** The agent walks onto the dock near the ferry's stop (-1084, -32) while the box is active, which the quest script gives as the ferry's arrival at waypoint 1 until it reaches waypoint 2. Live runs did not measure the start and end of that window (§6). The script gives a box of x -1124 to -1044 and y -72 to 8, and the live east edge matched it (§6). The server fires on an outside to inside crossing at a client update, so a character that already stands inside when the ferry docks must step out and back in. No client code is involved.
- **After the zone.** The character arrives in `oot` and continues through the native dialogue with Narrik (`hail`, then the `travel to butcherblock` saylink).
- **Model.** Stage 1 keeps the rowboat model. Stage 2 replaces it for race 72 (§8).

## 5. Staging and test

Stage 1 is one stage. Unit tests cover the boat exemption of the bodytype filter (a boat is kept, a utility spawn of another race is still dropped), the refusal of a target, consider and hail that names an untargetable spawn, the nearest-NPC pickers that skip it, `is_boat` and `velocity` on `VisibleEntity`, and the additive wire fields, which do not bump the protocol version. Live: stand at the dock, watch the ferry appear in `visible` and dock, walk into the box, and arrive in `oot`.

Stage 2 is described in §8.

## 6. Discovery results

- **Confirmed live (2026-11-18, before stage 1).** The test character stood at the dock at (-1060, -30, -72) with nothing happening. After stepping east to x=-969 and walking back west it changed zone to `oot`, and the client had no ferry at any time. `interact.hail` on `SirensBane` returned "No NPC matching", so the ferry was never in the entity table. This shows that the trigger needs no deck and that the client dropped the ferry.
- **Visibility (live, stage 1 build).** The ferry shows in `visible` as `SirensBane000` with race `SHP`, `is_boat` true and a velocity. It appears at 485 to 498 units and drops out at 490 to 499 units, so the 500 unit range binds. Line of sight breaks near the dock, so the entry flickers at 56 to 121 units from a character on the street. From (-960, -30) the ferry appears only about 3 s before it docks. Before a `#repop`, `freporte` listed every static spawn twice, so `visible` listed `SirensBane000` and `SirensBane001` at identical positions with identical velocity in every observation. `oot` listed `Narrik` and `a_boat022` twice as well when the character arrived there without a `#repop`.
- **Route.** Nine of the 20 waypoints lie within 500 units of the dock, the farthest 1191 units away, from jimbo grid reads and not from the live run files.
- **Motion.** Cruise speed is 10.4 units/s (median over 83 samples, range 9.7 to 14.2 at waypoint turns), which is 8.3 units/s for each unit of the npc `runspeed` of 1.25. Docked reads 0.0 and the first update after the pause already reads full speed, so no ramp shows. Position updates arrive every 1.5 s (median over 76 intervals, longest 1.95 s at an observation step of 0.15 s), about 15.6 units apart.
- **Zero velocity at waypoints.** At 6 of the waypoints in range (2, 3, 5, 17, 18 and 19) the sampled velocity reads 0 for one update interval, with the ferry still cruising. The position repeats across two samples, which is consistent with a second update at the waypoint. `MOTION_STALE` (3 s) is not the cause, since it exceeds the longest update interval, and it stays unchanged.
- **Loop time.** The ferry reached the dock at 1795042571.8 and again at 1795042974.8 (epoch seconds), so the loop takes 403 s with one update interval of error. The pause from arrival to the first moved update is 61.9 s. The path is 3547 units, which at 10.4 units/s is 341 s and with the pause gives 403 s. Two longer intervals (468 s and about 450 s) each contain a stretch of 110 to 130 s with no character in `freporte`. In both, the extra time matches the absence minus 60 s to within 7 s, and the zone log reports "Zone has gone idle after 60 seconds". A third interval with a 36 s absence took about 400 s. The loop is likely to stand still in an empty zone after a 60 s grace, which was not tested further.
- **Trigger edge.** The box edge is x=-1044 as computed from the script. A character that settled at x=-1038.1 or x=-1035.3 did not trigger. In five walks the last client position in `freporte` before the zone change was x=-1045.7, -1048.7, -1051.1, -1055.1 and -1055.6, which is 1.7 to 11.6 units inside the edge. The walk that stopped at x=-1045.7 triggered 1.3 s after it stopped, with no further move. With two ferries spawned, the character arrives in `oot` 6 to 22.5 units east of its last client position (6.2, 12.8, 20.6, 20.7 and 22.5), so the server saves an older position than the client last reported. The earlier trigger at x=-1024.8 is probably this lag from a trigger at the box edge, but no walk triggered outside the edge, and the size of the lag is not explained. Only the east edge was walked, the other three were not.
- **Ferry away.** From x=-960 to x=-1149 in 10 unit steps along y=-30 (through the whole box, starting 22 s after the ferry left and crossing the edge 34 s after it) there was no zone change.
- **Already inside.** A character that stood inside the box when the ferry docked did not trigger, and neither did 25 steps inside it over 38 s. Entering from outside triggered at once. The box fires on an outside to inside crossing, so a rider must step out and back in.
- **z bound.** In the air3 run a character fell from z=25.6 at x=-1015 toward the floor (z=-72). The last `freporte` frames before the zone change read z=-18.41 at x=-1044.69 and then z=-34.48 and z=-39.03 further west, so the trigger came at a height of 33 to 54 units above the floor and 45 to 66 above the ferry (z=-84). The character arrived in `oot` with a saved z of -1.12, an older position than the last client update. The box has no z bound at those heights, and the script gives none (`eq.set_proximity` with four arguments sets z to +-999999). Only air3 tests the z bound, since the air2 run crossed the edge at floor height (z=-70.56).
- **Duplicate portal.** With two ferries spawned, each trigger gave two `OP_RequestClientZoneChange` packets within 1 ms, which fits one cast of spell 2279 for each ferry. Two "/MQZone used" messages in `narrik.txt` fit two casts. The code reading is that the client answered both packets with `OP_ZoneChange` and that the server refused the second and cancelled the move. The zone log is the source for the "Invalid unsolicited zone request" line, and those log lines were not preserved. The character arrived in `oot` at the saved x and y of `freporte`, for example (-1038.3, -30, -1.1), and not at the spell destination.
- **Single ferry (live, after `#repop`).** A GM `#repop` in `freporte` replaced the two ferries with one (`SirensBane002`, spawn id 576). A walk from x=-1000 across the edge, started while the ferry was docked, changed the zone to `oot` and the character arrived at (-9000, 459, 8.5) with the ferry docked 5.8 s earlier. The spell destination was the arrival point, so the second request caused the wrong arrival. The client now answers one request for a destination within 5 s of the previous reply, and a refusal clears that record.
- **Hail by name.** The hail by name is refused as `hail_untargetable` with no Say and no target packet, which unit tests cover and no live run has tested. On the stage 1 build before that refusal existed, `interact.hail {"name": "SirensBane000"}` was accepted while the ferry was visible at 493 units. The client sent `Hail, SirensBane` as a Say with `target_id` null before and after. The `freporte` zone log has no `POSSIBLE_HACK` line, but it was not shown to record one and the run had no positive control, so this is weak evidence.
- **Narrik, live.** From a GM teleport to (-9190, 262) in `oot`, `interact.hail {"name": "Narrik"}` returns the saylinks "travel to Freeport" and "travel to Butcherblock". Choosing "travel to Butcherblock" gave "Off you go!", the message "Gmkblr's Portal to Butcher lands on Gmkblr" and an arrival in `butcher` at (3112, 856, 7.9) after 6.3 s.
- **End to end.** The single ferry run continued from the arrival at (-9000, 459, 8.5). A hail there gave no dialogue, since Narrik stands at (-9202, 262), 282 units away. A straight walk of 8.7 s reached (-9199, 265), the hail then returned both saylinks, and "travel to Butcherblock" arrived in `butcher` at (3112, 856, 7.9) after 6.2 s. The whole ride from the crossing to `butcher` took about 65 s and needed no deck, no waiting for the ferry and no GM command.
- **Confirmed by code.** `turning` never fires for this ferry, and the native client carries riders by continuous reprojection (§2).
- **Deck, `SHIP`.** The mesh has 662 vertices and 532 polygons, all solid, with bounds x -64 to 197, y -113 to 110 and z -38 to 131 around the model origin. Upward facing surfaces sit at z = -2 and z = -13, and the hull bottom at z = -38. Whether the native client collides against this mesh is not confirmed, and no authored hull exists. The shi and row boats have authored hulls.
- **Not confirmed.** Whether the native client keeps the bodytype 11 ferry visible by the same rule (taken from how the game plays, not from the decompile). The y and west edges of the box. The exact start and end of the active window, which live runs bound only coarsely, with triggers 5.8 s to 43.2 s after the ferry reached the dock and none 34 s after it left. Whether the double spawn returns after a zone or server restart. The client reply guard has unit tests only, because the single ferry removed the duplicate request that would exercise it.

## 8. Stage 2: ship model and deck

**Model.** `eq_race_to_code` returns `BOT` for races 73, 141, 502 and 533 and `SHP` for every other boat race, including 72. `SHP` selects the archetype `ship`, drawn from `ship.glb` (extracted from `shi.eqg`), at `archetype_scale` 0.5. `BOT` keeps `boat`. The ship is a static, unskinned galleon whose bow is the model's +x.

**Deck.** `ShipDeck` reads the triangles of `ship.glb` once, the first time a ship is in view. It keeps the upward facing, level triangles (normal y at least 0.7, corners within 1 model unit of one height) whose height is between 20 and 60 model units, which is the main deck, the stern deck, the bow deck and the bowsprit. Crow's nests (height 91 and up) and sloped rails are left out. Each frame a `Boat` surface is placed with the renderer's own model matrix (`entity_model_matrix_static` with the ship's smoothed position and heading), so what is drawn is what can be walked on. Its id is `0x10000 + spawn_id`, which cannot collide with a door id. A rider on it appears as `own.standing_on` and in `Observation.surfaces` with `kind` Boat and `carries_riders` true.

On the real `ship.glb` the deck is 293 triangles, spans world z 11.99 to 22.94 above the ship's origin at the 0.5 scale, and its main deck lies 12 above the water, flush with the dock at 12 above the ferry's cruise height. An ignored test pins this (`the_real_ship_deck_is_flush_with_the_dock`, needs `$EQOXIDE_ZONE_ASSETS`).

**Carry.** A lift needs no carry, because the controller re-queries the floor each frame. A ship moves horizontally, so `BoatCarry` moves the rider by the ship's motion since the last frame (a rotation about the previous ship position by the heading change, then a translation to the new position) before `CharacterController::step`. Height is not carried, since the floor probe does that. The carry applies only when the previous frame ended standing on the same ship, and it ignores a jump larger than 50 units, which is a server move and not a sail. Both the carry and the publish run in `app.rs` after the entity smoothing and before the controller step.

**Merged layer.** `Collision::set_surfaces` replaces the whole surface layer, so lifts and ships are published together by `SurfacePublisher`, which rebuilds when a lift key or a ship pose changes.

**Live result.** The ship deck is published and visible to the agent (`surfaces` holds the ferry as `kind` Boat). A carried ride cannot be shown on this ferry. The server's boarding box (x -1124 to -1044, y -72 to 8) covers the deck (x -1096 to -1066, y -63 to 9 at the dock), and the trigger fires on the first client update inside it, so a character that reaches the deck, by walking or by `#zone`, is already on its way to `oot`. A rider is therefore never carried on this ferry. The carry matters for a boat whose rider is not moved by a trigger. It is covered by unit tests and by three controller tests (`boat_ride_tests`: a rider sails with the ship, a rider off the deck is left behind, a rider turns with the ship).

**Not built.** Boat-local offsets on the wire, board and leave messages, and any steerable boat. Whether the native client collides against the `SHIP` mesh in the zone archive is still not confirmed (§6).
